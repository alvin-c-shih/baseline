The Night Line: Sequences

Text, audio and code are sequences: variable length, order matters. Verse 2 is a night train, one word per car, and four generations of machinery ride along it. You'll build each one and score them on the same held-out Shakespeare. Then you'll compare BERT with GPT, and swap in the parts that changed between the 2017 Transformer and today's LLMs.

2.1–2.2

Tokens, and a window three tokens wide

The oldest language model just counts which word follows which, and runs out of examples fast.

A familiar idea first

A Markov-chain text generator

Many developers have written the toy version: a hash map from the last two words to counts of the next word, then sample from it. That's a trigram model. Embeddings fix its blindness: give each token a short list of numbers, like coordinates on a map, so similar words sit near each other and share what was learned.

Text becomes integers via a tokenizer; real LLMs use BPE sub-word tokens. An n-gram model counts how often each token follows each short context. Longer windows help until the table goes sparse. Counting can't tell that "the king" and "the queen" are alike. Embeddings can.

The math
$$P(c\mid\text{ctx})=\frac{\mathrm{count}(\text{ctx},c)+k}{\mathrm{count}(\text{ctx})+kV}$$
LAB · notebook §2.1–2.2
Back then

Speech recognition and machine translation ran on n-grams for decades. The trick was more data and cleverer smoothing (Kneser–Ney, 1995), not more linguistics.

The business side

Counting statistics ran Google Translate from 2006 to 2016, and the predictive text on phone keyboards. More web text beat smarter rules, an early version of "scale wins."

Lore

Frederick Jelinek of IBM is credited with "Every time I fire a linguist, the performance of the speech recognizer goes up" (the exact wording is disputed). In 2006 Google released a trillion-word n-gram dataset under the blog title "All Our N-gram are Belong to You."

2.3 · 1990

RNN: carry a hidden state

An RNN reads one token at a time and keeps a running summary of everything so far.

A familiar idea first

A fold with an accumulator

h = reduce(f, tokens, h0). An RNN is a fold over the sequence where the accumulator h is a vector and f is learned. Like any fold, step t can't start until step t−1 finishes.

A recurrent network updates a fixed-size hidden state at each step. To train it, unroll it over the sequence and backprop through every step: BPTT.

The math
$$h_t=\tanh(W_{hh}h_{t-1}+W_{xh}x_t+b)$$
LAB · notebook §2.3
Back then

RNNs were elegant on paper and miserable in practice: slow, sequential, unstable, and forgetful past a few dozen steps.

The business side

Sequential means slow to train. You can't spread one sequence across thousands of GPU cores, and that cost ceiling is what attention later broke.

Lore

Karpathy's 2015 post "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks" had a character-level RNN writing fake Shakespeare, fake Linux kernel code and fake algebraic geometry in LaTeX. The Tiny Shakespeare file in your lab comes from that project.

2.3

Gradients vanish or explode

Sending the error back through many steps multiplies it by the same matrix again and again, so it either dies or blows up.

A familiar idea first

Compound interest

0.9⁶⁴ ≈ 0.001 and 1.1⁶⁴ ≈ 450: multiply by the same factor 64 times and you get almost nothing or a huge number. With a matrix, the "factor" is how much it stretches or shrinks vectors.

If the recurrent matrix shrinks vectors, the gradient vanishes; if it stretches them, the gradient explodes. Explosion has an easy fix, gradient clipping. Vanishing limits plain RNNs to short memories.

LAB · notebook §2.3
Back then

A training run could look fine for hours and then print loss = nan after a single exploding step. Clipping (Pascanu et al., 2013) became standard practice.

Lore

Bengio, Simard and Frasconi's 1994 paper is titled "Learning long-term dependencies with gradient descent is difficult." It's one of the field's most honest titles.

2.4 · 1997

LSTM put gates along the road

An LSTM keeps a separate memory lane with learned gates that decide what to forget, write and show.

A familiar idea first

An accumulator with keep / write / read dials

Instead of overwriting the running summary every step, an LSTM keeps a separate total and three learned dials: how much of the old total to keep, how much of the new item to add, and how much to show. Adding to a total instead of replacing it is what lets information survive.

The cell state runs along the sequence like a highway. Three gates (forget, input, output) control it. Updates are multiply-and-add instead of a squash through a matrix, so gradients can survive hundreds of steps. That's an LSTM.

The math
$$c_t=f_t\odot c_{t-1}+i_t\odot\tilde c_t\qquad h_t=o_t\odot\tanh(c_t)$$
LAB · notebook §2.4
Back then

Published in 1997 and largely ignored for a decade, LSTMs became the industry default around 2015, in Google's speech recognition and Translate, Apple's QuickType and Amazon's Alexa.

The business side

LSTMs were the first deep sequence models in products used by billions: Google voice search (2015) and Translate (2016), plus keyboards and voice assistants at Apple and Amazon.

Lore

The original 1997 LSTM had no forget gate; Gers, Schmidhuber and Cummins added it in 2000. So the part the film's barriers call F arrived three years after the paper.

2.5 · 2014

Seq2seq and the bottleneck

Squeeze a whole sentence into one vector, then unpack it into another language. That works until sentences get long.

A familiar idea first

A fixed-size summary

Imagine summarizing any document, a tweet or a novel, into exactly 64 numbers, then asking someone else to translate it from the summary alone. Short inputs survive; long ones lose detail. It's lossy compression to a fixed size, whatever the input length.

For translation, an encoder reads the input into one vector and a decoder writes the output from it. A 5-word and a 50-word sentence must fit in the same space: the bottleneck. In the lab, accuracy collapses as strings get longer.

LAB · notebook §2.5
Back then

Seq2seq (Sutskever, Vinyals and Le, 2014) was the first end-to-end neural translator to rival phrase-based systems, which had decades of engineering behind them.

The business side

End-to-end learning replaced translation pipelines of dozens of hand-built components with one model. That meant one thing to maintain instead of a stack of specialist systems.

Lore

One of the paper's key tricks was to feed the source sentence in backwards, which put the first words close to where decoding starts. Your lab's reversal task honors it.

2.5 · 2014

Attention: look back and double-check

Instead of relying on one summary vector, the decoder looks back at every input word and decides which ones matter now.

A familiar idea first

A soft dictionary lookup

A dict lookup compares a query with the keys and returns the one exact match. Attention compares the query with every key, scores each by similarity (a dot product), turns the scores into percentages that sum to 100% (the softmax), and returns that blend of the values.

Attention scores the decoder state against every encoder state, turns the scores into weights with a softmax, and reads the weighted average. The bottleneck disappears. Your α matrix shows the same anti-diagonal as the film's floor.

The math
$$\alpha_{tj}=\operatorname{softmax}_j(s_t\cdot h_j)\qquad \text{context}_t=\sum_j\alpha_{tj}h_j$$
LAB · notebook §2.5
Back then

Long sentences were translation's biggest weakness. Attention (Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio, 2014) fixed it, and Google shipped neural translation (GNMT) in 2016.

The business side

Google switched Translate to neural attention models in late 2016 and reported large error reductions on major language pairs, one of deep learning's first visible wins in a consumer product.

Lore

Attention weights were the field's first popular "look inside the model" picture. Then came the papers "Attention is not Explanation" (2019) and, months later, "Attention is not not Explanation."

2.6 · 2017

Transformers ran in parallel

Drop the recurrence: let every position attend to every other at once, and the GPU does it all in parallel.

A familiar idea first

A loop vs a parallel map

A loop whose iterations each need the previous one's result can't run in parallel. A map over independent items can. Attention computes every position's output straight from the inputs, with no carried state, so all positions run at once on a GPU.

A Transformer layer is self-attention plus a per-position MLP, with residuals around both. A GPT-style model adds a causal mask so each position sees only the past. TinyGPT in blcourse.py is about 50 lines.

The math
$$\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\Big)V$$
LAB · notebook §2.6
Back then

RNNs process tokens one after another, so training couldn't use a GPU's thousands of cores. Transformers trained faster, on more data, and that turned out to be what mattered.

The business side

Parallel training made GPUs and Google's TPUs pay off, and the Transformer became the architecture the AI industry is built on. All eight authors later left Google, and most went on to found or join AI companies.

Lore

"Attention Is All You Need" spawned hundreds of "X is all you need" papers, which is the joke behind every pre-chorus in this song.

2.6

The scoreboard

At laptop size the LSTM and the Transformer tie. The Transformer wins by scaling, not by being smarter when small.

A familiar idea first

Average surprise

Cross-entropy measures how surprised the model is by the real next character, averaged over the text. ln(65) ≈ 4.17 nats is a blind guess among 65 characters. eloss, the perplexity, reads as "like choosing among this many equally likely options": a loss of 1.5 is like choosing among about 4.5.

n-grams, LSTM and Transformer, on the same validation text, in nats per character of cross-entropy. Module 5 measures what does separate them: how loss falls as you add compute.

LAB · notebook §2.6
Back then

By 2018, recurrent networks were disappearing from new state-of-the-art results in translation and language modeling.

Lore

Recurrence didn't stay gone. Linear-time RNN-like models (RWKV, Mamba) came back in 2023 to challenge attention, which is Module 5's ending.

2.7 · 2018

BERT read both ways

BERT reads the whole sentence at once, both directions, and learns by filling in hidden words.

A familiar idea first

Fill-in-the-blank vs autocomplete

A cloze test ("the cat sat on the ___ and purred") lets you read both sides of the gap. Autocomplete only sees what came before. BERT trains on the first kind of task, GPT on the second.

BERT is a Transformer encoder trained by hiding words behind [MASK]. It's great for classification and search, but it can't write. The lab asks a real DistilBERT to fill the film's mask.

LAB · notebook §2.7
Back then

BERT topped nearly every NLP benchmark at once in late 2018. Researchers spent 2019 fine-tuning it on everything, and studying it, a subfield people jokingly called "BERTology."

The business side

In 2019 Google began using BERT in Search, on about 1 in 10 US English queries, and called it one of the biggest search changes in five years.

Lore

ELMo (2018), then BERT, then ERNIE, then Big Bird: for a while NLP model names were mostly Sesame Street characters. RoBERTa, ALBERT and DistilBERT kept the joke going.

2.7 · 2020

Decoder-only scaled up

A next-word predictor, made big enough, does most of what BERT did, and it can also write.

A familiar idea first

One general tool vs a drawer of scripts

Like replacing a drawer of single-purpose scripts with one general tool you steer with instructions. Each script may still win at its own job, but one tool is far easier to ship and maintain.

A decoder-only model uses a causal mask; that mask is the only architectural difference from BERT. Encoders live on as embedding models for search.

LAB · notebook §2.7
Back then

GPT-2 (1.5B, 2019) and GPT-3 (175B, 2020) showed generation quality improving steadily with size. The field's center of gravity moved from fine-tuning to prompting.

The business side

OpenAI sold GPT-3 as an API in 2020: pay per token, no training required. Models became a metered utility, and a whole layer of "AI applications" was built on top.

Lore

In 2019 OpenAI initially withheld the full GPT-2 as potentially "too dangerous to release," then released it in stages over nine months. Today it runs in a browser tab.

2.8 · 2021

Sinusoids gave way to RoPE's rotation

Rotate each query and key by its position, so attention only sees how far apart two tokens are.

A familiar idea first

Clock hands

Hands at 3:00 and 5:00 are as far apart as hands at 9:00 and 11:00: the angle between them depends only on the difference. The dot product of two vectors depends on the angle between them. So rotate each vector by its position, and the score depends only on how far apart two tokens are, not where they are.

Attention is order-blind, so position must be injected with a position encoding. RoPE rotates vectors by an angle proportional to position, so their dot product depends only on the difference. The lab checks it: positions (5, 2), (105, 102) and (1005, 1002) give the same score.

The math
$$\langle R_m q,\ R_n k\rangle = \langle q,\ R_{n-m}k\rangle$$
LAB · notebook §2.8
Back then

Models trained on 2K tokens fell apart on 4K. Position schemes that generalize to longer inputs were the bottleneck for long context, and RoPE variants (with "scaled" frequencies) became the standard fix.

The business side

Context length became a product feature with a price tag. Stretching RoPE to longer inputs (position interpolation, YaRN) let companies extend existing models instead of retraining from scratch.

Lore

RoPE was first published by Su Jianlin on his Chinese-language blog before the RoFormer paper. A blog post became the position encoding in most open LLMs.

2.8 · 2019–20

LayerNorm → RMSNorm, ReLU → SwiGLU

Two small parts swapped for cheaper or better ones, and nobody can fully say why the new ones work better.

A familiar idea first

Auto-levelling audio

A mixing desk keeps every stage's volume in range so the next stage is neither overdriven nor inaudible. LayerNorm removes the offset and sets the volume; RMSNorm only sets the volume, and that turns out to be enough.

RMSNorm drops LayerNorm's mean subtraction: cheaper, and as good. SwiGLU replaces the MLP's ReLU with a learned gate. A real LLM's config lists silu, rms_norm_eps and rope_theta: the verse's parts list.

The math
$$\mathrm{RMSNorm}(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{\tfrac1d\sum x_i^2+\epsilon}}\odot g$$
LAB · notebook §2.8
Back then

Normalization layers were essential and poorly understood. BatchNorm's original "internal covariate shift" explanation was challenged in 2018, and in 2017 Ali Rahimi's NeurIPS talk compared the field to alchemy.

The business side

Small savings per layer compound. An operation done in every layer, for every token, of every request shows up directly in the serving bill.

Lore

Noam Shazeer's SwiGLU paper ends: "we attribute their success, as all else, to divine benevolence." It's the field's most-quoted shrug.

Checkpoint

  1. Why does an n-gram model stop improving as n grows?
    Answer

    The table goes sparse: most long contexts in new text never appeared in training, so their counts say nothing, and counting can't share what it learned between similar words.

  2. What goes wrong when you backprop through 64 RNN steps, and how do LSTM gates help?
    Answer

    The gradient is multiplied by roughly the same matrix at every step, so it vanishes or explodes. The cell state is updated by multiply-and-add through gates, so with the forget gate near 1 the signal passes through almost unchanged.

  3. What is the seq2seq bottleneck, and how does attention remove it?
    Answer

    The whole input must fit in one fixed-size vector. Attention lets the decoder look at every encoder state at every step and take a weighted blend.

  4. Why is a Transformer easier to scale than an LSTM, even at equal loss?
    Answer

    Every position is computed in parallel, so training uses a GPU fully and grows with more data and compute. An RNN must process tokens one after another.

  5. BERT vs GPT: what is the one architectural difference?
    Answer

    The attention mask. BERT sees both directions (good for understanding and embeddings); GPT sees only the past (good for generating).
